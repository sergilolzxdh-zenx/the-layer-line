Retraction has two settings that matter: **distance** (how far the filament is pulled back) and **speed** (how fast it's pulled). Guessing at both together makes it hard to know which one actually fixed — or caused — a problem. A printed retraction tower tests distance methodically while holding everything else constant.

## Know your extruder type first

This changes your starting point more than anything else:

- **Bowden** (motor mounted away from the hot end, filament pushed through a PTFE tube): typically needs 3–7 mm of retraction distance, because some of the pulled-back filament is absorbed by compressing slightly in the tube before the effect reaches the nozzle.
- **Direct drive** (motor mounted directly above the hot end): typically needs only 0.5–2 mm, since there's minimal tube length for filament to compress into.

Starting from the wrong range wastes test prints. If you don't know which your printer has, check the extruder: if the filament travels through a length of tube before reaching a separate hot end assembly, it's Bowden.

## Printing a retraction tower

1. Download or model a test object with multiple separate towers or pillars on a single print bed, connected only by travel moves. Several free retraction test models are widely available for common slicers.
2. Slice it with retraction distance increasing at set intervals up the height of the print, if your slicer supports built-in retraction towers, or print several versions at fixed distances (for example 1 mm, 2 mm, 3 mm, 4 mm, 5 mm for a Bowden setup) if it doesn't.
3. Examine the result: strings should visibly decrease as distance increases, then plateau. The lowest distance where stringing disappears (not the highest distance overall) is your target: going further adds risk without benefit.

## Then tune speed

Once distance is set, retraction speed determines how fast that filament actually gets pulled back before the travel move starts:

- Too slow, and the nozzle starts moving before retraction finishes, so the effective retraction is less than what you configured.
- Too fast, particularly on flexible filaments like TPU, can strip the filament against the drive gear instead of pulling it back cleanly.

A reasonable starting point for most rigid filaments is 25–45 mm/s; increase in small steps and watch for stripped filament (a visible chewed or flattened spot) as the upper limit.

<Callout kind="tip" label="Retest after any hot end change">
Retraction distance is partly a function of how much empty space exists between the extruder gear and the melt zone. Replacing a nozzle, hot end, or PTFE tube can shift that distance even if nothing else changed. It's worth a quick retest, not a full assumption that old settings still apply.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="mistake">
Tuning retraction on PLA and assuming the result transfers directly to PETG or TPU. Viscosity when molten varies enough between materials that retraction distance and speed are genuinely filament-specific, not just printer-specific. See the [stringing guide](/print-quality/stringing/) for why PETG tends to need more attention here, or the [TPU guide](/materials/tpu-guide/) for why flexible filament often needs less retraction, not more.
</Callout>

## Typical retraction ranges by material

These are starting points for a retraction tower, on a Bowden setup — direct drive generally needs roughly a third to a half of these distances, per the extruder-type guidance above:

| Material | Distance | Speed | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| PLA | 4–6 mm | 40–60 mm/s | Most forgiving; wide working range |
| PETG | 4–7 mm | 30–45 mm/s | Prioritize distance tuning over speed; see [PETG guide](/materials/petg-guide/) |
| ABS/ASA | 3–5 mm | 40–60 mm/s | Similar to PLA; less prone to stringing than PETG |
| TPU | 0–2 mm | 20–30 mm/s | Often printed with retraction disabled entirely — see [TPU guide](/materials/tpu-guide/) |

## Complementary settings once retraction is dialed in

Retraction distance and speed solve most stringing on their own, but a few slicer settings work alongside retraction rather than replacing it:

- **Coasting** stops active extrusion a short distance before a travel move begins, letting existing nozzle pressure finish the last bit of the line instead of the retraction move having to counteract it. It's a smaller, complementary effect, not a substitute for correct retraction distance.
- **Wipe on retract** moves the nozzle slightly along the last printed line while retracting, which helps hide any oozing that happens during the retraction move itself rather than preventing it.
- **Linear advance / pressure advance** (firmware-dependent naming) adjusts extrusion pressure dynamically based on print speed changes, which reduces blobbing at corners and can indirectly reduce the amount of retraction needed by keeping nozzle pressure more consistent throughout the print. This is a firmware-level tuning pass distinct from retraction and worth exploring once basic retraction is solved, not before.
