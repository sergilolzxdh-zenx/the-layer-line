# Niche Research

How "desktop FDM 3D printing troubleshooting & calibration" was chosen over 20 other candidate niches for The Layer Line.

**Method:** each niche is scored 0–100 on ten criteria, based on general market knowledge, category reasoning, and a small number of live searches used to sanity-check the finalist (see "Verification" below) — not on a licensed keyword-volume tool. Where a number isn't backed by a verifiable source, treat it as a labeled estimate for relative comparison between niches, not an absolute figure. Scores are the author's judgment calls, made to compare niches against each other, not a scientific measurement.

**Criteria:** Traffic Potential, Monetization, Competition (higher = more favorable to a new entrant, i.e. *less* saturated), Evergreen Potential, Content Depth, Long-tail Opportunities, AdSense Potential, Tool Potential, Originality Potential, AI-Overview Resistance (higher = harder for a one-line AI answer to fully satisfy the search).

## Why 3D printing won

It isn't the highest score on any single axis — dog training and pickleball have more raw search volume, insurance/finance niches (deliberately excluded above) have higher CPC. It wins on the combination that matters for a **new site with no existing authority**:

- **Tool potential (90) is the standout.** Filament cost, print time, retraction thresholds, and single-wall flow tests are all genuinely calculable, not decorative — a new site can out-tool incumbent forum threads and old blog posts immediately, without years of backlinks.
- **AI-Overview resistance (85).** "Why is my print stringing" isn't a one-line answer — the real answer branches on extruder type, material, and printer history, which is exactly the kind of query where a full diagnostic page beats a generated snippet. Compare to "what's the ideal watering schedule for a pothos," which an AI Overview answers completely on its own.
- **Content depth and long-tail (85/85).** Calibration alone supports a genuine pillar-and-cluster structure (E-steps → flow rate → retraction → bed leveling) where each piece is a real prerequisite for the next, not an artificially split single article.
- **Competition (70) favors a new entrant** more than most consumer hobby niches: the two dominant historical resources (Simplify3D's troubleshooting guide, manufacturer knowledge bases) are useful but static and not built as a browsable, cross-linked site with calculators — a real gap, not a crowded field of competing publishers.
- **No YMYL exposure, no fabricated-credential risk.** Unlike dog training or fitness, correct FDM troubleshooting doesn't require professional certification to write credibly — it requires correctly explained, well-established engineering principles, which is a standard this site can actually meet.

Monetization (60) and traffic (65) are middling, not exceptional — 3D printing hardware and filament are commercial-intent adjacent (CPC is real but not insurance-tier), and the hobbyist audience, while growing, is smaller than dog owners or home cooks. That's a deliberate trade against the categories this site can actually win in as a new publication.

## Verification (live checks, not exhaustive)

Three live searches were used to sanity-check this pick before committing (full results are in the research trail of this project, not reproduced here to avoid stale numbers going out of date in this file):

- Confirmed the desktop/hobbyist 3D printing segment is growing (industry sources put desktop printer shipment growth in the ~15%/year range as of 2025), not shrinking — ruling out "declining hobby" risk.
- Confirmed Simplify3D's and Prusa's troubleshooting resources are real, current, and exactly as dominant as assumed — both are cited directly in this site's content rather than duplicated without attribution.
- Checked that "The Layer Line" wasn't already an existing 3D printing publication or an obvious trademark collision before committing to the brand name.

## What would change this decision

If Search Console data after launch (see `CONTENT_ROADMAP.md`) shows print-quality/calibration terms getting materially less real traffic than the estimates above assumed, materials content (higher CPC-adjacent, broader audience) is the most likely pivot within the same niche rather than abandoning it — the content, tools, and internal linking already built would mostly carry over.
